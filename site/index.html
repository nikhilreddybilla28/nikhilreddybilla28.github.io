Applied AI Scientist, Agentic AI · SAP Labs, Palo Alto

I'm an applied scientist on the agentic AI team at SAP Labs. I work on LLM agents that turn natural-language requests into OData and REST queries across S/4HANA, Ariba, and SuccessFactors. Day to day, that means building the evaluation framework and the synthetic training data for those agents, and digging into why an agent picks the wrong tool or grounds an argument badly.

Until May 2026 I was a master's student in Computer Engineering at Virginia Tech and a graduate research assistant in Ruoxi Jia's Responsible Data Science (ReDS) Lab. My research there was on LLM safety and privacy. One project showed that new jailbreaks mostly recombine older attack tactics, and used that to train defenses that hold up on attacks the model has never seen (ICLR 2026). Another pulled verbatim training data out of Llama models by driving them into a high-entropy state (EMNLP 2025).

Before grad school I spent two years at NCR Voyix in Hyderabad putting ML into production, mainly anomaly detection over system logs and the MLOps pipeline on Amazon EKS that retrained and redeployed those models. I also did computer vision research at IIIT Hyderabad and, as an undergrad, at NIT Rourkela, where I earned my B.Tech in Electrical Engineering.

Adversarial Déjà Vu: Jailbreak Dictionary Learning for Stronger Generalization to Unseen Attacks

Mahavir Dabas, Tran Huynh, Nikhil Reddy Billa, Jiachen T. Wang, Peng Gao, Charith Peris, Yao Ma, Rahul Gupta, Ming Jin, Prateek Mittal, Ruoxi Jia

ICLR 2026 International Conference on Learning Representations

Most "new" jailbreaks recombine tactics from earlier ones. We extracted 16,901 adversarial skills from 32 jailbreak papers, and sparse dictionary learning reduces them to a dictionary of 397 primitives. Training on compositions of those primitives (ASCoT) gives an open-weight 8B model robustness comparable to Claude Sonnet-4-Thinking. It also beats o4-mini on most seen and unseen jailbreak families, with competitive over-refusal.

Retracing the Past: LLMs Emit Training Data When They Get Lost

Myeongseob Ko, Nikhil Reddy Billa, Adam Nguyen, Charles Fleming, Ming Jin, Ruoxi Jia

EMNLP 2025 Main conference, pp. 35316–35337

Paper arXiv
BibTeX

  author    = {Ko, Myeongseob and Billa, Nikhil Reddy and Nguyen, Adam and
               Fleming, Charles and Jin, Ming and Jia, Ruoxi},
  booktitle = {Proceedings of the 2025 Conference on Empirical Methods in
               Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)},
  pages     = {35316--35337},
  year      = {2025}

An LLM that is about to recite memorized text usually shows a sustained spike in token-level entropy first. The Confusion-Inducing Attack (CIA) optimizes an input snippet to recreate that state and reaches a 22.2% verbatim extraction rate on Llama-2 70B. Aligned models resist it, so for them we first fine-tune on mismatched question-answer pairs, which weakens alignment enough for the attack to land.

Data is All You Need (Almost): Iterative Synthetic Instruction Tuning for Secure Code Generation

Team HokieTokie, Virginia Tech (faculty advisor: Ruoxi Jia)

Nova AI Challenge 2025 Amazon Nova AI Challenge proceedings, Trusted AI track

Paper

We took a pretrained CodeGen model with no safety training and taught it to write secure code mostly by fixing its data. Taxonomy-guided synthetic instructions came first, and after each tournament round we patched whatever the red teams had broken. My part was evaluation and fine-tuning: 13 LLM-as-a-judge harnesses built around CodeQL, Amazon CodeGuru, and Semgrep, and LoRA/QLoRA and GRPO runs that traded code utility against security.

CNN based image resizing forensics for double compressed JPEG images

Nikhil Reddy Billa, Bibhash Pran Das, Mrutyunjay Biswal, Manish Okade

Detects whether a JPEG was resized between its two compressions and estimates the resize factor. A preprocessing layer of 20 fixed high-pass filters suppresses scene content, so the CNN learns from resampling residuals. It outperformed four earlier methods on RAISE images on both tasks.
